Can you attend the Cannes Film Festival

Not freely: the Festival sells no tickets to the general public, and access is reserved for accredited film professionals and press. Three doors exist, however: the 3 Days in Cannes program for ages 18 to 28, Cannes Cinéphiles, and the Cinéma de la Plage, free and open to all every evening.

The Cannes Film Festival sells no tickets to the general public: official screenings run through an online ticketing system reserved for accredited attendees only, and the red-carpet ascents are by invitation, in evening dress. The 80th edition will be held from May 11 to 22, 2027; registration opens around early February on festival-cannes.com for film professionals and press, with proof of professional activity (in 2026: from February 2 to April 15, a surcharge of €224 including tax after April 1, and an environmental contribution of €20 excluding tax). Three doors exist for everyone else. The 3 Days in Cannes program, free upon application, is for ages 18 to 28. Cannes Cinéphiles reserves around 3,000 free accreditations each year for active film lovers, film students, and school groups, with access to screenings from the Official Selection, Critics' Week (Semaine de la Critique), and the Directors' Fortnight (Quinzaine des Cinéastes); applications go through the online accreditation portal, with the 2027 calendar not yet published. Finally, the Cinéma de la Plage offers open-air screenings every evening of the Festival, free and without accreditation. One trap to anticipate: accommodation, to be booked 6 to 12 months ahead, as Croisette hotel rates multiply by 3 to 5 during the Festival's 12 days.
